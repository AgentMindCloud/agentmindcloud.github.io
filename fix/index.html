1. Getting in

Sign in with the Cursor account

Grok Bot uses your Cursor account. There is no separate Grok Bot login.

Sign in to Grok Bot

Desktop, from How do I sign in to Grok Bot on desktop?:

  1. Open the Grok Bot app.
  2. Choose the sign-in button the app shows.
  3. Finish signing in in your browser with your Cursor account.
  4. Return to Grok Bot. You should see your account in Settings.

If your Grok Bot access comes from a seat on a self-serve Grok Business plan, choose Sign in with Grok in the browser and use the Grok account that holds the seat.

Sign in to Grok Bot

Mobile, from How do I sign in to Grok Bot on mobile?:

  1. Open the Grok Bot mobile app.
  2. Sign in with the same Cursor account you use on desktop.

Which plans include access

Grok Bot access is included on every paid individual Cursor plan and on Cursor Teams. You can also grant usage to your Cursor account by linking an individual SuperGrok, SuperGrok Plus, SuperGrok Heavy, or X Premium+ subscription, or by signing in with a Grok account that has an assigned seat on a self-serve Grok Business plan.

Sign-in still fails after update, cache clear, and a private window

From Why is Grok Bot showing a persistent authentication error even after I log out, clear cache, and reinstall?:

Start with these steps, which resolve most persistent authentication errors:

  1. Update the desktop app to the latest version, then sign out and sign back in.
  2. Clear browser cache and cookies for cursor.com and x.ai, then sign in again in a private or incognito window.
  3. On desktop, also try signing in at x.ai/bot using the same Cursor account.
  4. If the error started recently, wait 30 to 60 minutes and retry. Some auth errors clear on their own as your session refreshes.

Reinstalling the app usually does not fix this, so try the steps above first.

2. Computer stuck on "still starting up"

The live recovery page does not use the words "still starting up." It also does not give a time after which a computer with no Recover control is failed. Use the steps it does publish.

The Grok Bot computer is shared by your bots. Files, logins, installed apps, and the browser live there.

Grok Bot computer

Open computer controls from Settings > Updates. You can also get Recover from the reconnect screen when the app offers it.

Grok Bot computer

Try these, in the page's order

From Which should I try first?:

  1. Wait, then choose Retry if the computer says Reconnecting or Couldn't Reach Grok Bot's Computer. Bots, files, and logins are safe while it reconnects. Do not Reset during this.
  2. Fully quit Grok Bot and reopen it. On Mac, choose Quit from the menu bar, not just close the window.
  3. Update the computer from Settings > Updates if an update is offered.
  4. Recover if it still cannot reconnect, or if an update looks stuck and Recover computer is shown. On a slow update, Keep waiting is the safe default.
  5. Reset only after those steps. Like Recover, it can lose very recent changes if the computer can't be reached to save them.

Do not start a second Update or Reset while one is already running. Repeated resets can interrupt a restore that is still finishing.

Grok Bot computer

The member controls on that page are Update, Recover, and Reset. It does not list a Restart button, and it does not tell you to reinstall the desktop app.

How long the page says to wait

From How long should I wait?:

  • Reconnecting and Recover usually take a few minutes. You can choose Continue in Background.
  • An Update can also take a few minutes when it is downloading a large image or transferring data. If it says Update still running, keep waiting.
  • Choose Recover only if the update appears stuck and the app offers Recover computer. If recovery is not available, keep waiting.
  • If Recover or Reset fails, use Retry Recovery or Retry Reset once. Then stop and contact support.

"A few minutes" is the only duration that page states, and it applies to Reconnecting, Recover, and an Update that is downloading or transferring. "If recovery is not available, keep waiting" has no end time. This guide does not add one.

Recreate and Terminate are an Enterprise admin control

Each member of your team gets one hosted computer where every Bot they run does its work.

Manage Grok Bot computers

Grok Bot Computers is Enterprise only, and it appears only for organization admins. Team admin rights aren't enough, because one computer spans every team the member belongs to. Members never see this control.

Manage Grok Bot computers

On that admin screen, Terminate's "when to use it" cell says: "End a member's current work, or clear a computer that is stuck." Recreate and Terminate are dashboard actions for organization admins. They are not a Restart or Recreate button on every plan. If you are not an Enterprise organization admin, you will not see them. Use the member steps above.

When recovery is not available, email support

If you have no Recover or Reset control, the recovery page says to keep waiting. It does not publish a cutoff. If Recover or Reset fails, use the retry once, then stop.

Email hi@cursor.com. The recovery page says to include your Cursor account email, Grok Bot version, which action you already tried, the agent name, and a screenshot of the computer status. The get-help page is the one that states the address: "Email hi@cursor.com."

The live pages do not mention a computer ID field in Settings, and they do not use the status words "computer unavailable." Send the screenshot of the computer status instead of inventing an ID.

3. Usage limits and routines that skip

This section is what the live pages let you do today. It is not a per-chat or per-routine usage breakdown.

The bot says you hit a usage limit

No. Your weekly usage is used up. It resets each week. To keep going now, turn on on-demand usage or move to a bigger Cursor plan.

Grok Bot FAQs, "My Bot stopped replying and says I hit my usage limit. Is it broken?"

They are two separate meters for two products. Grok Bot usage lives on your Cursor account. See it in Grok Bot under Settings > Usage & Billing, not in the Grok app.

Grok Bot FAQs, "Why doesn't my SuperGrok limit match my Grok Bot usage?"

Paid Grok Bot access includes Weekly usage. That included grant resets weekly. When it runs out, extra Grok Bot usage can continue as On-demand usage if on-demand is enabled. That extra usage is billed through Cursor and counts toward the On-demand monthly limit.

Plans and billing

If on-demand is off, Grok Bot stops when weekly usage runs out. The screen says you have reached your Grok Bot usage limit, and it resets with weekly usage.

Plans and billing

To turn on-demand on, that page says:

  1. In Grok Bot, open Settings and find On-demand monthly limit. Choose Enable if it is off.
  2. If Grok Bot asks for a card, finish that on the web. In a browser, go to cursor.com/dashboard, open Spending, and set Monthly Limit.
  3. If you subscribed in the iOS or Android app, set that limit on the web.

Weekly usage in Grok Bot is included usage. It resets weekly. A SuperGrok, SuperGrok Plus, or SuperGrok Heavy title on that meter is the linked grant, not a second Cursor plan.

Plans and billing

The pages say to look at Weekly usage under Settings > Usage & Billing. They do not say that screen shows a percentage.

Routines that spend usage, and routines that skip

If Weekly usage is falling faster than you expect, pause routines you don't need. Then ask the Bot to widen schedules and narrow Slack triggers before you raise a plan or an on-demand limit. A routine that never runs doesn't spend usage. Test does, because it performs the work.

What the live pages do not document

No page fetched on 2026-10-08 describes an automatic catch-up of a routine that did not run because weekly usage ran out. Why hasn't my routine run? says "Check these first" and then only these lines:

  • The routine isn't Paused. On the phone, Active is on.
  • When to run shows the schedule or trigger you expect, in the right time zone.
  • A new schedule waits for its next time. Use Test to run it now.
  • For Slack, the message is new, in the channel the trigger watches, and matches its phrase.
  • For a webhook, the call returned 200 and used the current key.

That list does not mention a usage limit, and it does not say missed runs replay after the weekly reset.

No page describes a per-routine usage meter. The routine row shows name, schedule or Paused, and the fields above. It does not show how much usage that routine spent.

Until the product pages say otherwise, the documented next steps are:

  1. Open Grok Bot Settings > Usage & Billing and read Weekly usage. It resets each week.
  2. Pause routines you don't need. Ask the Bot to widen schedules and narrow Slack triggers.
  3. Turn on on-demand usage, move to a bigger Cursor plan, or wait for the weekly reset.
  4. After usage is available again, run the missed work yourself with Test, or ask the Bot in chat. Test spends usage. Nothing on these pages replays the skipped run for you.
